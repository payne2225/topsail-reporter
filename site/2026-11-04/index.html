Calm after the blow, sunny and 76: trout on live shrimp through the afternoon flood

The first truly quiet morning after a long stretch of wind: 65 at dawn, sunny with a high near 76, an east breeze of 6 to 9 mph with gusts no better than 16, and seas of 3 to 4 feet from the east that will keep the small boats inside. The sound is the play on a neap tide: the Hampstead station bottoms out at 10:14 AM, floods to 4:07 PM and ebbs through sunset at 5:14 PM, with surf-zone water at 69 at the buoy and the inside water cooling toward the trout. The Fisherman's Post November report, posted Sunday, has speckled trout showing from the creeks to the piers, red drum to 32 inches off the north-end piers, and kings that were strong before the blow. Nothing new for rod-and-reel anglers from NCDMF since the last edition, both sounds are open for oysters, and the shellfish waters should stay that way: there has been no measurable rain since October 17.

The Bite

Best bet: the sound and the creeks, the afternoon flood from 10:14 AM to 4:07 PM, then the start of the ebb to dark. The Hampstead station falls from a 3:39 AM high of 3.8 feet to a 10:14 AM low of 0.5 and climbs back to 3.9 feet at 4:07 PM, a swing of about 3.3 feet under a 28 percent waning crescent, the slack end of the cycle before the new moon next Monday builds the tides again. The current will be moderate, which suits a cork. The wind is the story: east-northeast at 6 mph at dawn, east at 7 to 8 mph through the middle of the day with gusts to 16, east-southeast at 8 by evening, the lightest day in weeks. An east wind runs across Topsail Sound rather than down it, so the open waterway will be ruffled but fishable, and the creeks that leave the ICW toward the mainland will be glass. For whoever is up at first light: sunrise is 6:33 AM and the water is falling from the 3:39 AM high until 10:14 AM, the morning the guides have been waiting for. Work a topwater plug along the grass lines and the creek mouths while the light is low, then switch to a cork once the sun is up. Through the afternoon, fish the flood as it fills the creeks: live shrimp under a slip cork or popping cork, set to hang a foot or so off the bottom in 4 to 8 feet of water, over oyster points, dock pilings and the seam where a creek's current meets the waterway. From the 4:07 PM high through sunset at 5:14 PM the first of the ebb pulls bait out of the marsh, and the last hour of light is when the trout feed hardest.

What was reported this week, from the Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry report for November, posted Sunday, November 1, three days ago. Joe at East Coast Sports: in the sound, "anglers are catching smaller reds and occasional speckled trout," and the focus should shift to trout "as the weather continues to cool." Capt. Ricky Kellum of Speckled Specialist Charters: many small speckled trout around, with some to 28 inches; float live shrimp under a cork in 4 to 16 feet, most often 4 to 8, and focus on structure and current; for artificials, 3/16 ounce jig heads with soft plastics such as a Slick Jr.; black drum to 18 inches over structure on floating shrimp, and the small reds take the same bait. A 27 inch trout on live shrimp from the New River was the report's photo. Mike of Native Son Guide Service: "bait has been pushed around by this long stretch of winds," the water "has just started to cool with the colder evenings, so stable weather should put the area into trout mode"; target the main creeks and waterways and "the deeper seams (5-8')"; the reds inside "are the same schools that have been around all summer," artificials will find them but "cut bait will likely produce the best results," and "the bite should only get better as the surf zone fish move back inside." Adam of Carolina Flats Fishing: topwater early to locate the trout schools, then the deeper holes later; the reds should feed in a "last blitz" before the bait thins, on oyster and grass banks with soft plastics such as Gulp; two Hampstead anglers doubled on bluefish near Topsail casting topwater along grass lines. Today is the stable weather Native Son asked for: no wind to speak of, 76 degrees, and the Masonboro buoy at 69 this morning, down from the mid 70s in mid October. The Beaufort gauge, the nearest inside reading, 60 miles up the coast, sits at 66.

Typical for the first week of November, from the November 1, 2025 report: migratory trout of 16 to 21 inches staging around the inlets while the resident fish move up the creeks, live shrimp under a slip cork "a top producer," lighter jig heads upriver and heavier ones near the inlet, reds solid with 25 to 28 inch fish mixed in, and black drum on drifted shrimp. The Topsail.Fish weekly roundup of October 28 (a website's summary, its only dated source an October 22 king report) rates red drum the top inshore bet at creek mouths, docks and oyster edges on live or cut mullet, and trout "rising" at creek mouths, oyster points and current seams, with the advice to slow the retrieve as the water cools and to change depth before changing spots.

For the young angler: how to hook a live shrimp for the cork. Run the hook once through the shrimp from the side, under the hard ridge on top of its head, in front of the dark spot you can see through the shell. Behind that spot is the brain; miss it and the shrimp kicks for an hour, hit it and you are fishing a dead one. Set the cork so the shrimp hangs just above the oysters or the bottom, cast up-current of the point or piling, pop the cork once, then let it drift and sit. When the cork goes under and stays under, reel until the rod loads, then lift. Trout have soft mouths, so no hard jerk and no high sticking at the boat or the bank.

The inlet. By boat, the coastal waters forecast for Surf City to Cape Fear is east 10 knots with seas of 3 to 4 feet, an east swell of 4 feet at 8 seconds, going southeast 5 to 10 tonight and southeast 5 knots Thursday: no advisory, but a lumpy bar on an east swell for a small skiff, and the ebb runs against that swell from the 3:31 PM ocean high onward. Thursday looks better, southeast 5 knots on the same seas. The piers and the nearshore boats in the November report have the Spanish and bluefish feeding around bait and structure, false albacore in the area "hopefully" (Native Son) and feeding with the Spanish (South End Adventures), kings at 8 to 12 miles and on the 50 to 60 foot structure, and bluefish, albacore and gray trout on the nearshore reefs on diamond jigs, small epoxy jigs or soft plastics (Plan 9). On foot, the south end is open: the Topsail Nature Preserve stays open to anglers walking in from the South End lot, on a leash for the dogs, no vehicles. The ocean station at the Ocean City pier is high at 3:10 AM, low at 9:16 AM, high at 3:31 PM and low at 9:42 PM. A Gotcha plug or a small metal jig at the inlet beach around the afternoon high, and a chunk of mullet on the bottom in the deeper water on the ebb. Bush Marina in Topsail Beach is closed this week for ramp maintenance (town notice, posted October 16, "beginning Monday, November 2, 2026, for the week"), so trailer boats use the Surf City ramp on Roland Ave; the Wildlife Commission lists no ramp closures in Pender or Onslow (page updated October 29).

The surf. The surf zone forecast is gone for the season (NWS Wilmington ended it October 31, back April 1), so there is no official rip current rating today; the Masonboro buoy read 3.3 foot seas at 8 seconds from the southeast at mid-morning and the marine forecast says 3 to 4 feet, so expect a working surf on a light onshore wind, and wade accordingly. Water 69 at the buoy. The November report says "the occasional speckled trout can be found in the surf" (East Coast Sports), and the Wrightsville Beach report for November, the nearest busy reporting area 25 miles south, posted the same day, has slot red drum in the surf with a chance at a big one, whiting mixed with them, a small push of pompano expected as the water clears with "sand fleas the best bait option," and black drum picking up around hard structure on fresh shrimp (Intracoastal Angler). The play on this beach: a two-hook bottom rig with fresh shrimp, sand fleas or Fishbites in the trough for sea mullet and pompano, a fish-finder rig with a chunk of mullet for a red, and a Gotcha plug for the blues when they show on the bait. Ocean lows 9:16 AM and 9:42 PM, highs 3:10 AM and 3:31 PM; no source ties the Topsail surf bite to a tide stage. The Topsail Fall Surf and Pier Fishing Challenge fishes from Friday midnight to noon Sunday, surf division bluefish, trout, black drum and sea mullet (organizer page, read today), so the beach will have company by the weekend.

The piers. The piers post their daily catches only on Facebook, which this report cannot read, so the freshest word is Sunday's. Robin at Jolly Roger Pier: "the pier has started seeing some scattered speckled trout showing up," bottom fishing "better at night, with catches of good-sized Virginia mullet and spot," and "bluefish have been pretty thick, especially in the early mornings." Vinita at Surf City Pier: bottom fishing "has produced a mix of pompano, black drum, and croakers," casting chances for Spanish and bluefish, and "king mackerel fishing was really good before this last blow, and hopefully the king bite isn't done just yet." East Coast Sports adds that the piers "have been catching a few big red drum (32"+), especially on the north end of the island," and that the kings were strong "with double-digit daily catches" before the wind. So: a Gotcha plug at first light for the blues, a bottom rig with shrimp, bloodworms or Fishbites through the day for the mullet, pompano and croaker, and a chunk of mullet or a live bait on the bottom after dark for a big red, which must go back if it is over 27 inches. With the wind down and seas easing, a king rig is worth a day on the end. Today's word: Jolly Roger 910-328-4616, Surf City 910-328-3521, Seaview 910-328-3172. Jolly Roger is $16 a day, kids 13 and under half price, two rods, blanket licence included (pier page, read today); Surf City Pier is open 24 hours, $12 for one rod or $24 for two, good 24 hours from purchase, parking free while fishing, blanket licence included (pier site, read today); Seaview says "Open 24/7" (read today), $17 a pass (pier house page, October 23). Pier anglers in the weekend tournament: "No pin-rigging (no anchor rods)" is a tournament rule, and the pier division is bluefish, red drum, black drum and sea mullet.

Wrightsville Beach, for context. Beyond the surf notes above, the November report there has the trout bite "becoming more predictable" in the creeks off the ICW and the deeper holes near the inlet (Coastline Fishing Charters), reds "very good" on natural bait around hard structure along the ICW, bull reds on the nearshore structure and the jetties, sheepshead strong on fiddler crabs, kings "on fire" along the beachfront on live or dead bait, and false albacore "among the better November options," cast metals or a clouser into the surface frenzies (Intracoastal Angler); Johnnie Mercer's Pier had pompano to 3 pounds and more and big reds to 42 inches on some nights. Nothing dated this week on false albacore off Topsail itself; the two Topsail boats that mention them say "in the area" and "hopefully."

Regs Corner

  • New on the proclamations index since the last edition, all commercial: FF-49-2026 (October 30) closes the commercial croaker fishery from December 16, FF-50-2026 (October 30) closes commercial spot from January 1, FF-48-2026 (October 27) closes a commercial flounder mobile-gear area, and M-25-2026 (October 27) closes gill-net management units south of Albemarle Sound. None changes a recreational limit (all six index pages read today).
  • Shellfish waters: both sounds open. The newest polluted-area proclamation is still PA-67-2026 (October 16, Brunswick and Carteret). Stump Sound has been at normal boundaries since October 16 (PA-66-2026) and Topsail Sound since October 9 (PA-61-2026). Rain closes them; Friday's forecast is for less than a tenth of an inch. Hotline 252-515-5656.
  • Oysters are in season in open waters: hand harvest from public bottom, 1 bushel per person per day, 2 per boat, 3 inch minimum, cull on site, hands or hand rakes only, sunrise to sunset, sanctuaries off limits (SF-1-2026). No licence is needed for recreational shellfish.
  • Flounder: closed. The 2026 season was September 1 to 14 only (FF-27-2026). Release every one, including the ones that eat your red drum bait.
  • Speckled troutthree a day, 14 to 20 inch slot, one over 26 allowed. Red drum: one a day, 18 to 27 inches; the 32 inch pier fish in this week's report all go back. Black drum: ten a day, 14 to 25 inches, one over 25. Bluefish: five a day. Spot and croaker: fifty a day each. Striped bass from the sound, the waterway or the New River cannot be kept at all. If you keep a red, a trout or a weakfish, report it at deq.nc.gov/report-my-fish.
