## Text
Boats stay in today, a 7 foot swell under a Small Craft Advisory, so fish the sound: the Hampstead tide floods from 10:09 AM to 4:21 PM and drops through dusk, and red drum on a live finger mullet at the grass lines and docks are the call. ## Lead
The first cool morning of the fall: 48 degrees at dawn, sunny with a high near 74, a north wind around 14 mph gusting to 22 on land and 15 to 20 knots gusting 25 on the water, where a Small Craft Advisory runs through Wednesday evening with seas of 5 to 7 feet. The sound is the play: the Hampstead low is 10:09 AM, the flood runs to the 4:21 PM high, and the ebb carries through sunset at 6:29 PM to the 10:53 PM low, on a neap tide with a gentle swing. Red drum are the fish of the month in the creeks, the ICW and the bays, speckled trout are the fish to hope for as the water cools, and every flounder goes back. Both sounds are open for oysters, and nothing new has come from NCDMF since the oyster proclamation on October 2.

## The Bite
**Best bet: the sound and marsh, flood tide from 10:09 AM to 4:21 PM, out of the wind.** The Hampstead station bottoms out at 10:09 AM at 0.9 feet and tops at 4:21 PM at 3.8 feet, a neap swing under a 70 percent waxing gibbous moon, so the current is gentle and the water sits on the grass a long time around the top of the tide. The wind is the problem: north at 14 mph gusting to 22 all day, with the stronger gusts before noon. A north wind comes off the mainland, so the calm water is tucked against the mainland bank and up the creeks where the marsh breaks it, and the island side of the sound takes the chop. Work the creek mouths, grass lines and docks north of the inlet, up Banks Channel and into the creeks, with a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig or a Gulp shrimp under a popping cork; cut mullet on the bottom on a day the wind keeps the fish from looking up. The last two hours of the flood into 4:21 PM, then the first of the ebb at the creek mouths through sunset at 6:29 PM, are the windows.

What was reported: the Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry report for October was posted on October 1, nineteen days ago, and the category index this afternoon shows nothing newer; the November report is expected around the 1st. In it, Spring Tide Guide Service said reds were schooled in the bays more than in a normal summer pattern, most 22 to 28 inches, taking topwater and artificials some days and cut bait on others. Native Son Guide Service had mullet everywhere, reds at docks, creek mouths and grass lines, and only a few scattered trout early on topwater, with the creeks "still a bit early". Speckled Specialist Charters called the red drum fishing good on Carolina-rigged live mullet, trout okay in the first hour of light on a Heddon Spook Jr. and later on live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet, and black drum of 3 to 4 pounds on shrimp around bridges and oyster reefs. Carolina Flats Fishing had big mullet in the ICW and bigger reds on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks, with flounder as bycatch. A 12 year old angler was pictured with a 24 inch flounder from a Hampstead-area dock; flounder harvest is closed, so every one goes back. On Captain Experiences, a Shallotte-based guide's note from four days ago (the site gives only relative dates, and Shallotte is 60 miles down the coast) called the trout bite "off and on" and named fresh shrimp, cut mullet, Vudu shrimp and paddle tails under popping corks over oyster beds and grass lines near points with current.

Typical for this week, from last year's November 1 report: the first hard north winds of the fall pushed trout toward their wintering creeks earlier than usual, migratory trout of 16 to 21 inches staged around the inlets, and reds stayed solid with 25 to 28 inch upper-slot fish mixed in; live shrimp under a slip cork was the top trout producer. Today is the first north wind of that kind, and with the Masonboro buoy at 72 degrees and the surf zone around 70, the water is where the October 1 report said the trout bite would turn on. No one has reported it yet.

For the young angler: a Carolina rig is the fall red drum rig every report names. Slide a 1 ounce egg sinker up the main line, add a small bead, tie on a swivel, then 18 inches of 20 pound leader to a 2/0 circle hook. Hook the finger mullet through the lips, cast to the edge of the grass, and let it sit with the rod in your hand. With a circle hook you do not jerk: when the line pulls tight and the rod bends, just reel and lift, and the hook sets itself in the corner of the mouth, which is why reds on a circle hook almost always swim off well.

**The inlet.** Not by boat today: the marine forecast for Surf City to Cape Fear is north 15 to 20 knots gusting 25 with seas 5 to 7 feet, an east swell of 7 feet every 12 seconds, under a Small Craft Advisory through Wednesday evening; Thursday eases to 3 to 5 feet and Friday to 1 to 3 feet of surf. On foot, the south end is open: the Topsail Nature Preserve stays open to anglers walking in from the South End lot (dogs on a leash, no beach driving), and Spring Tide reported Spanish "pushing inside the inlet on a high tide" on October 1. The ocean high at Topsail Inlet is 3:42 PM and the sound high 4:21 PM, so mid afternoon is the hour at the mouth with a Gotcha plug or a small metal jig, casting with the wind at your back toward the south. With this much swell running the inlet, keep well back from the edge of the bar.

**The surf.** Surf is 3 to 5 feet, rip current risk Moderate, water around 70 in the surf zone, UV index Moderate. Ocean tides: low 9:08 AM, high 3:42 PM, low 10:06 PM. The swell is the story: 7 feet of east groundswell at 12 seconds against a north wind, which grooms the face and digs out the sloughs. If you fish it, that means 4 to 6 ounces of sinker, a two-hook bottom rig with fresh shrimp, sand fleas or Fishbites for pompano, sea mullet and spot, and a chunk of mullet on a fish-finder rig for a red or a bluefish; the October 1 report had slot and over-slot reds, Spanish and bluefish along the beach and pompano and Virginia mullet on the bottom. No source ties the Topsail surf bite to a tide stage, so take the ocean times as they are. Thursday and Friday, with 1 to 3 foot surf and a lighter wind, will be the better surf days, and Saturday the rip current risk goes to High again on a northeast wind. Do not wade out onto the bar today.

**The piers.** The piers post their daily catches only on Facebook, which this report cannot read, so these are from the October 1 report, nineteen days old. Jolly Roger: baitfish everywhere and the king bite turned on with multiple kings to 32 pounds, Spanish getting bigger, night bottom fishing best for red drum to 31 inches, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish, black drum and some flounder. Surf City Pier: good sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot reds with an occasional over-slot, evening mixed bags of black drum, mullet, spot and croaker. Seaview Pier: "the mullet migration is starting", Spanish and bluefish on Gotchas or diamond jigs, and a bottom mix of flounder, sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, speckled trout, black drum and the odd red. Last October Seaview said kings were waiting for water below 76 degrees; the buoy reads 72 today. For today's word, call: Jolly Roger 910-328-4616, Surf City 910-328-3521, Seaview 910-328-3172. Surf City Pier is open 24 hours, the grill 7 AM to 10 PM on a Tuesday (pier site, read today), $12 for one rod or $24 for two, good 24 hours, blanket licence included (fees page, October 14); Jolly Roger is $16 a day with kids 13 and under half price and holds a blanket licence too (pier page, October 14).

**Wrightsville Beach, the nearest busy reporting area, 25 miles south.** Its Fisherman's Post October report (October 1; nothing newer on the index today) had pompano, sea mullet and scattered reds on bottom rigs in the surf, Spanish and blues better from the piers, kings to 33 pounds starting to show, Spanish 1 to 3 miles out and false albacore arriving 5 to 8 miles out, with trout slow. A search today for false albacore off Topsail or Wrightsville turned up nothing dated this month. Anyone driving over: the Heide Trask drawbridge had an emergency lane closure Thursday October 15 and WECT's headline on October 19 said it was down to one lane again for inspections, so expect delays.

## Regs Corner

No rain is forecast this week, so they should stay that way; the 24 hour hotline is 252-515-5656.
- **Oysters** are open in open waters: hand harvest from public bottom, 1 bushel per person per day, 2 per boat, 3 inch minimum, cull on site, hands or hand rakes only, sunrise to sunset, sanctuaries off limits (SF-1-2026). No licence is needed for recreational shellfish.
- **Flounder: closed.** The 2026 season was September 1 to 14 only (FF-27-2026). Release every one.
- **Red drum:** one a day, 18 to 27 inch slot. **Speckled trout:** three a day, 14 to 20 inch slot, one over 26 allowed. **Striped bass** from the sound, the ICW or the New River cannot be kept at all. If you keep a red, a trout or a weakfish, report it at deq.nc.gov/report-my-fish.
