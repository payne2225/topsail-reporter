## Text
Flat calm sound at first light with the tide coming in until late morning: throw topwater for reds and trout along the grass lines and creek mouths, then switch to a live finger mullet once the sun is up. Surf is small and the rip risk is low. Oysters open at sunrise Thursday, with Topsail Sound open and Stump Sound still closed. The wind takes the morning off, so the pinfish will have to do the stealing themselves.

## Lead
A near windless, sunny Wednesday on the island, with the sound tide rising from 5:11 AM to 11:28 AM and the ocean topping out at 10:22 AM. Red drum are the fish of the month in the sound and surf, Spanish are still around the inlet and the piers, and flounder are release-only. Oyster season opens at sunrise Thursday in open waters, and the first real cold front of the fall arrives Saturday with north wind and a high near 69.

## The Bite
**Best bet: the sound and marsh at first light.** Wind is west at 4 mph at dawn and the water will be glass. Sunrise is 7:15 AM and the Hampstead tide has been flooding since the 5:11 AM low, so the water is coming up onto the grass through the whole morning until the 11:28 AM high. Start with a topwater plug (a Heddon Spook Jr. was the named lure) at the creek mouths and grass lines north of the inlet, then go to a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig, or a Gulp shrimp under a popping cork, when the sun gets high. The afternoon drops out from 11:28 AM to the 5:58 PM low, which is the dock and creek-mouth window as bait flushes out; sunset is 6:37 PM with the tide just turned back in. Moon is a 12 percent waxing crescent, so the swing is middling, not a big spring tide.

What was reported: the Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry report for October was posted on October 1, so it is two weeks old, and no newer one is up. In it, Spring Tide Guide Service said reds of 22 to 28 inches were the common catch and were "more schooled in the bays than usual", taking topwater and artificials some days and cut bait on others. Native Son Guide Service had reds at docks, creek mouths and grass lines, mullet "abundant", and a few speckled trout on topwater early. Speckled Specialist Charters had trout on a Spook Jr. in "the first hour or so of light" and later on live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet of water, plus 3 to 4 pound black drum on shrimp around bridges and oyster reefs. Carolina Flats Fishing had reds in the ICW on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks, with flounder as bycatch. Every flounder goes back: the harvest season is closed.

Typical for mid October, from last year's October 1 report: reds around docks, grass banks, shallow bays and the backs of creeks, trout starting to move toward the inlets over 6 to 8 foot drop-offs, and calm mornings being the best topwater mornings. That lines up with this morning.

For the young angler: a Carolina rig is a half ounce egg sinker slid on the main line, then a bead, then a swivel, then about 18 inches of 20 pound leader to a circle hook. Hook the finger mullet up through the lips, cast it to the edge of the grass, and leave the rod in the holder; with a circle hook you do not swing, you just reel until it loads up.

**The inlet.** Spring Tide reported Spanish mackerel "pushing inside the inlet on a high tide" (October 1). The ocean high at New Topsail Inlet is 10:22 AM and the sound high is 11:28 AM, so late morning is the time to be at the mouth with a small metal or epoxy jig or a Gotcha plug, casting at any bait being pushed up or birds working. Plan 9 Charters had Spanish of 14 to 22 inches just outside the inlet in as little as 15 to 20 feet, and Carolina Flats said false albacore should show first around structure within a couple of miles of the beach; as of October 1 they were "expected soon", and no one has reported them off Topsail since. Seas are 2 feet on a southwest wind of 10 knots today, a fine small-boat day. Friday afternoon it is not: southwest 15 to 20 knots gusting 25, then west 20 to 25 knots gusting 30 Friday night with seas 3 to 5 feet.

**The surf.** Surf is 1 to 2 feet, the rip current risk is Low, and the Masonboro buoy reads 77 degrees. Light southwest wind all day, building to a gusty 18 mph by evening. The October 1 report had slot and over-slot reds from the surf and the piers, Spanish and bluefish along the beach, and pompano and Virginia mullet on the bottom; Seaview Pier said "the mullet migration is starting". A live finger mullet on a Carolina rig is the red drum bait every fall report names, and a two-hook bottom rig with shrimp, sand fleas or Fishbites covers pompano and sea mullet. No report ties the Topsail surf bite to a tide stage, so take the ocean times as they are: high 10:22 AM, low 4:51 PM, high 10:39 PM. The Topsail Nature Preserve at the south end reopened October 7 and stays open to anglers on foot; dogs on a leash, no beach driving.

**The piers.** The piers post their daily catches only on Facebook, which this report cannot read, so these are from the October 1 report, two weeks old. Jolly Roger: kings to 32 pounds, red drum to 31 inches, Spanish, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish and black drum, with "anglers bottom fishing seeing the best success at night". Surf City Pier: good sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot reds with the occasional over-slot, black drum, mullet, spot and croaker, bottom fishing best in the evening. Seaview Pier: Spanish and bluefish on Gotchas or diamond jigs, sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, speckled trout and black drum. For today's word, call: Jolly Roger 910-328-4616, Surf City 910-328-3521, Seaview 910-328-3172. Jolly Roger and Surf City hold blanket licences, so you do not need your own on the pier; Surf City is $12 for one rod or $24 for two, good 24 hours, and Jolly Roger is $16 a day with kids 13 and under half price (pier pages checked October 14).

**Wrightsville Beach, the nearest busy reporting area, 25 miles south.** Its Fisherman's Post October report (also October 1) had trout holding in 4 to 6 feet most of the day, topwater at first light then live mullet; reds at docks on both the rising and falling tides; flounder at creek mouths on the rise and at grass lines and oyster beds at the top of the tide (release them here too); sheepshead at deeper docks at low tide; false albacore 5 to 8 miles out; and a 33 pound king from Johnnie Mercer's Pier.

## Beach & Weather
Sunny and warm, high near 83, low around 66 tonight, no alerts. The wind is the story: west at 4 mph at dawn, south-southwest at 6 by midday, 10 mph mid afternoon, then southwest gusting to 18 after dark. Surf is 1 to 2 feet, rip current risk Low, and the UV index is High, so sunscreen before the fish, not after. Water is in the mid 70s in the surf zone and 77 at the buoy. Sun up 7:15 AM, down 6:37 PM. Shorts and a long sleeve sun shirt will do today.

Thursday repeats it: sunny, 84, surf about a foot, low rip risk, southwest wind around 5 mph. Friday stays sunny near 83 but the southwest wind builds to 17 mph with gusts to 25 and seas 2 to 3 feet, then it swings west and blows 20 to 25 knots overnight. Saturday is the first cold front of the fall: north wind 18 to 22 mph, a high near 69, a 40 percent chance of showers, and a low near 49 Saturday night. Sunday is northeast at 21 mph, high 66, with the rip current risk up to Moderate and surf 2 to 3 feet. Dig out a sweatshirt by the weekend.

## Regs Corner
- **Oysters open Thursday at sunrise** (SF-1-2026, signed October 2, the newest proclamation on the NCDMF list this morning). Hand harvest from public bottom in open internal waters: 1 bushel per person per day, 2 per boat, 3 inch minimum shell, cull on site, hands or hand rakes only (rakes up to 12 inches wide), sunrise to sunset only, and oyster sanctuaries are off limits. No licence is needed for recreational shellfish.
- **Which water is open:** the NCDMF polluted area list this morning shows Topsail Sound reopened at sunrise October 9 (PA-61-2026) and Stump Sound closed since 8 a.m. October 11 (PA-63-2026) with no reopening posted. Rain closes these quickly; the 24 hour hotline is 252-515-5656.
- **Flounder: closed.** The 2026 harvest season was September 1 to 14 only (FF-27-2026). Every flounder from the surf, the piers and the sound goes back.
- **Red drum:** one fish a day, 18 to 27 inch slot. **Speckled trout:** three a day, 14 to 20 inch slot, with one over 26 allowed. If you keep a red, a trout or a weakfish, report it at deq.nc.gov/report-my-fish; that has been required since December 1, 2025.
- **Striped bass** from the sound, the ICW or the New River cannot be kept at all.
- Nothing new has been issued on the NCDMF current proclamations page since SF-1-2026 on October 2.

## Parting Shot
The spot that start showing on the piers this month are the big ones: NCDMF's species profile says the larger spot move into the nearshore ocean as the fall spawn draws near. A two-hook bottom rig with a piece of bloodworm is all it takes.
