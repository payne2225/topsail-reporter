The Bite

Best bet: the sound and marsh on the rising tide. The Hampstead low was 6:30 AM and the water comes up onto the grass all morning to the 1:05 PM high, then falls to the 7:29 PM low. The moon is a 28 percent waxing crescent, so this is a neap tide: a smaller swing and gentler current, which means the water stays on the grass longer around the top of the tide. The wind is the catch, southwest at 16 mph gusting to 25, so tuck into the creeks and the lee of the marsh banks rather than fighting it on the open bays. Start at the creek mouths and grass lines north of the inlet, up Banks Channel and the creeks, with a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig or a Gulp shrimp under a popping cork. The ebb from early afternoon through 7:29 PM is the creek-mouth and dock window as bait flushes out; the last of it runs at dusk.

What was reported: the Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry report for October was posted on October 1, so it is now fifteen days old, and the category index this morning shows nothing newer. In it, Spring Tide Guide Service said reds were schooled up in the bays rather than scattered, with quality fish mostly 22 to 28 inches, taking topwater and artificials some days and cut bait on others. Native Son Guide Service had mullet everywhere and reds responding at docks, creek mouths and grass lines, with speckled trout only just starting on early-morning topwater. Speckled Specialist Charters called the red drum fishing good on Carolina-rigged live mullet, with trout in the first hour or so of light on a Heddon Spook Jr. and later on live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet of water, plus 3 to 4 pound black drum on shrimp around bridges and oyster reefs. Carolina Flats Fishing had large mullet moving into the ICW and bigger reds active, most on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks, with flounder as bycatch. A 12 year old angler was pictured with a 24 inch, 5.73 pound flounder from a Hampstead-area dock on a finger mullet; every flounder goes back now, the harvest season is closed.

Typical for mid October, from last year's October 1 report: reds around docks, grass banks, shallow bays and the backs of creeks, trout starting to move toward the inlets over 6 to 8 foot drop-offs, and calm mornings being the best topwater mornings. Today is not calm, so the mullet and the cork will outfish the plug.

For the young angler: a popping cork is a float with a cupped face that pops and chugs when you jerk the rod tip, and a drum hears it as other fish feeding. Tie about 18 inches of 20 pound leader below the cork to a jig head with a Gulp shrimp, cast it beside the grass edge, let it sit, then give the rod one sharp twitch and wait. Most bites come while it sits still after the pop, and the cork simply disappears. Reel until the rod loads before you lift.

The inlet. Spring Tide reported Spanish mackerel "pushing inside the inlet on the high tide" (October 1). The ocean high at Topsail Inlet is 11:53 AM and the sound high is 1:05 PM, so late morning into early afternoon is the time at the mouth with a small metal or epoxy jig or a Gotcha plug, casting at pushed-up bait and working birds. Plan 9 Charters had Spanish of 14 to 22 inches just outside the inlet in 15 to 20 feet of water, with a surprise mix of false albacore, and Carolina Flats said albacore should show first around structure a couple of miles off the beach; no one has reported them off Topsail since that October 1 report, and a search this morning found nothing newer. Seas are 2 to 3 feet on a southwest wind of 10 to 15 knots gusting 20 today, a workable small-boat day with the chop building to 3 to 4 feet tonight. The weekend is off: a Gale Warning is in effect from Saturday afternoon through late Sunday night, north winds 25 to 30 knots gusting 40 and seas 5 to 8 feet Saturday night and 7 to 10 feet Sunday.

The surf. Surf is 1 to 2 feet, the rip current risk is Low, the UV index is High, and the water is in the mid 70s in the surf zone and 77 degrees at the Masonboro buoy. Ocean tides: low 5:07 AM, high 11:53 AM, low 6:30 PM. The October 1 report had slot and over-slot reds from the surf and piers, Spanish and bluefish plentiful along the beach, pompano and Virginia mullet on the bottom, and Seaview Pier said "the mullet migration is starting". A live finger mullet on a Carolina rig is the red drum bait every fall report names, and a two-hook bottom rig with shrimp, sand fleas or Fishbites covers the pompano and sea mullet. No report ties the Topsail surf bite to a tide stage, so take the ocean times as they are. The Topsail Nature Preserve at the south end reopened October 7 and stays open to anglers on foot; dogs on a leash, no beach driving. Saturday the north wind arrives with showers, Sunday and Monday the rip current risk goes to High with surf of 2 to 4 feet Sunday and 4 to 7 feet Monday: stay out of the water those days, and if you fish the beach after the blow, watch the sloughs the swell carves.

The piers. The piers post their daily catches only on Facebook, which this report cannot read, so these are from the October 1 report, fifteen days old. Jolly Roger: the king bite had turned on with multiple kings to 32 pounds, Spanish getting larger, and night bottom fishing best for red drum to 31 inches, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish, black drum and some flounder. Surf City Pier: good sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot reds with the occasional over-slot, and evening bottom fishing for black drum, mullet, spot and croaker. Seaview Pier: Spanish and bluefish on Gotchas and diamond jigs, with flounder, sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, speckled trout, black drum and the odd red on the bottom. For today's word, call: Jolly Roger 910-328-4616, Surf City 910-328-3521, Seaview 910-328-3172. Surf City Pier is open 24 hours, $12 for one rod or $24 for two, good 24 hours, and its blanket licence covers you (pier site, read this morning); Jolly Roger is $16 a day with kids 13 and under half price and holds a blanket licence too (pier page checked October 14).

Wrightsville Beach, the nearest busy reporting area, 25 miles south. Its Fisherman's Post October report (also October 1, nothing newer on the index this morning) had pompano, sea mullet and scattered reds on bottom rigs in the surf, Spanish and blues better from the piers, kings to 33 pounds starting to show, Spanish 1 to 3 miles out and false albacore arriving 5 to 8 miles out, with trout slow. Note for anyone heading that way Saturday: the IRONMAN 70.3 swim is in Banks Channel early Saturday morning (time in Around the Island) with the bike route through rural Pender County, and the Heide Trask drawbridge is down to one lane for emergency repairs (Port City Daily October 15, WECT October 15).

Regs Corner

  • Stump Sound shellfish waters reopened at sunrise today under PA-66-2026 (also Myrtle Grove Sound, Alligator Bay and Chadwick Bay), the newest item on the NCDMF polluted area list this morning. Topsail Sound has been open since sunrise October 9 (PA-61-2026). Rain closes these quickly, and this weekend brings rain, so check the 24 hour hotline, 252-515-5656, before you go Saturday or later.
  • Oysters opened at sunrise yesterday (SF-1-2026, signed October 2, still the newest proclamation on the NCDMF current list this morning): hand harvest from public bottom in open internal waters, 1 bushel per person per day, 2 per boat, 3 inch minimum shell, cull on site, hands or hand rakes only, sunrise to sunset, sanctuaries off limits. No licence is needed for recreational shellfish.
  • Flounder: closed. The 2026 harvest season was September 1 to 14 only (FF-27-2026). Every flounder from the surf, the piers and the sound goes back.
  • Red drum: one fish a day, 18 to 27 inch slot. Speckled trout: three a day, 14 to 20 inch slot, one over 26 allowed. If you keep a red, a trout or a weakfish, report it at deq.nc.gov/report-my-fish; that has been required since December 1, 2025.
  • Striped bass from the sound, the ICW or the New River cannot be kept at all.
  • Nothing new on the NCDMF current proclamations page since SF-1-2026 on October 2.
