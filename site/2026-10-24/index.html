Northeast blow, 8 foot seas: fish the sound on the afternoon flood, creek mouths first

A sunny, windy Saturday: 54 degrees at dawn, a high near 71, a northeast wind around 15 to 18 mph on land with gusts past 30 early and in the low 20s by mid afternoon, and on the water northeast 20 to 30 knots with seas of 6 to 8 feet under a Small Craft Advisory that runs through Monday evening. The sound is the only play: the Hampstead station tops out at 7:18 AM, ebbs to 1:48 PM and floods to 7:38 PM, on a spring tide under a 98 percent moon, with the Coastal Flood Advisory promising a little extra water on the marsh at the evening high. Red drum are the fish of the week in every report, speckled trout are the fish to hope for with the surf-zone water in the upper 60s, and every flounder goes back. Nothing new for anglers from NCDMF: the one proclamation signed this week is commercial, and both sounds are open for oysters.

The Bite

Best bet: the sound and marsh, the afternoon flood from 1:48 PM to 7:38 PM, in a creek out of the wind. The Hampstead station bottoms out at 1:48 PM at 0.6 feet and tops at 7:38 PM at 3.8 feet, a 3.2 foot swing on a spring tide (98 percent waxing gibbous moon), so the current runs hard and the water climbs well up onto the grass by the end of the light. The wind decides where: northeast at 15 to 18 mph with gusts to 34 at dawn, easing to gusts around 21 by mid afternoon, then back up after dark. A northeast wind runs straight down the length of Topsail Sound and the waterway, so the open ICW will be a chop; the creeks that leave the waterway toward the mainland are the sheltered water, and the marsh breaks the wind the farther in you go. Mid afternoon through sunset at 6:25 PM is the window: the gentlest wind of the day, two to three hours of rising water, and the reds moving up onto flooded grass and marsh edges as the tide tops out. Live finger mullet on a Carolina rig, or a Gulp shrimp under a popping cork, worked along the grass line and around dock pilings; a chunk of cut mullet on the bottom when the wind makes it hard to work a cork. For whoever is up at first light, the top of the morning tide is 7:18 AM (sunrise 7:23 AM) and the ebb runs until 1:48 PM: fish the creek mouths and drains on the falling water, where the current pulls bait out of the marsh, and dress for gusts past 30.

What was reported this week: Captain Mike Hoffman of Topsail Island Fishing Guides filed an inshore report on Topsail.Fish dated October 18, six days ago: red drum, speckled trout, black drum and sheepshead "are all getting in on the action", schooled and feeding on migrating bait in choppy, clear water behind a cold front, with a popping cork, soft plastics and topwater all drawing strikes and the advice to keep changing presentations through the day. "Fall fishing is here and the bite is heating up." His October 7 report said the reds were "pushing into very shallow water during the higher stages of the tide", with topwater while the light is low and live shrimp under a popping cork once the sun is up, and the trout bite improving with the cooling water. Topsail.Fish's own weekly summary of October 21, which leans on that October 18 report plus unnamed pier reports, rates red drum the top inshore bet in the creeks, at docks, on marsh edges and at the inlets, calls the trout "more consistent than in earlier October reports" at creek mouths, oyster edges and current seams, and puts black drum and sheepshead on oysters, bridges and pilings with fresh shrimp or fiddler crabs. It also says spot are showing at the piers but not yet as "a widespread, sustained run". Take that summary for what it is, a website's roundup, and the October 18 captain's report for what it is, one boat's week.

The Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry report for October was posted October 1, twenty three days ago, and the category index this afternoon shows nothing newer; the November report is expected around the 1st. Its sound notes still frame the month: East Coast Sports had red drum "the main target" from the ICW and the creeks to the inlets and no trout reports yet; Native Son had mullet everywhere and reds at docks, creek mouths and grass lines; Spring Tide had reds schooled in the bays, most 22 to 28 inches, on topwater and artificials some days and cut bait on others; Speckled Specialist had reds on "Carolina rigged live mullet", trout in the first hour of light on a Heddon Spook Jr. and later on live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet, and black drum of 3 to 4 pounds on shrimp around bridges and oyster reefs; Carolina Flats had bigger reds following big mullet into the ICW, on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks, with flounder as bycatch.

Typical for this week, from the November 1, 2025 report: the first hard north winds pushed trout toward their wintering creeks early, migratory trout of 16 to 21 inches staged around the inlets, reds stayed solid with 25 to 28 inch fish mixed in, and live shrimp under a slip cork was the top trout producer. The water is now where that pattern starts: the Masonboro buoy read 69 degrees this afternoon and the surf zone forecast says upper 60s, down from 72 at the buoy on Tuesday, and the Beaufort gauge, the nearest sound-side reading, 60 miles up the coast, sits at 65. This is the second northeast blow in a week on cooling water. If the trout are going to show at the creek mouths, this is the weekend.

For the young angler: how to read a creek mouth, from a Topsail.Fish how-to posted October 20. On a falling tide the water pours out of the marsh and carries shrimp and minnows with it, so the fish wait just outside the mouth where the current spreads out. Stand back from the mouth, cast up-current toward it, and let your lure or cork sweep along the edge of the flow. Look for a line of grass or foam drifting toward the channel: that is the current seam. The trout sit on the deeper side of that seam; the reds work the shallow edge and the little drains. If nothing is moving at slack tide, move to a dock or an oyster point until the water starts to run again.

The inlet. Not by boat: the marine forecast for Surf City to Cape Fear is northeast 20 to 25 knots gusting 30 this afternoon, 25 to 30 knots tonight and Sunday, seas 6 to 8 feet building to 6 to 9 feet, under a Small Craft Advisory through Monday evening that warns of seas up to 10 feet. On foot, the south end is open: the Topsail Nature Preserve stays open to anglers walking in from the South End lot (dogs on a leash, no beach driving, paid parking through October 31). The ocean high at the Ocean City pier station is 6:41 PM today and the low 12:37 PM; the Fisherman's Post October report had Spanish "pushing inside the inlet on high tide", but that was three weeks and a lot of wind ago. A Gotcha plug or a small metal jig cast down-wind from the inlet beach around the afternoon high is the only honest shot. The swell is running the bar; stay off it.

The surf. Surf is 2 to 4 feet, rip current risk Moderate, with a Beach Hazards Statement until 8 PM for a strong longshore current running north to south; water in the upper 60s; UV index Moderate. Ocean tides: high 6:23 AM, low 12:37 PM, high 6:41 PM. A northeast wind runs along this beach, so the current will carry a bottom rig down the beach fast: fish heavy, 4 to 6 ounces on a pyramid sinker, a two-hook bottom rig with fresh shrimp, sand fleas or Fishbites for sea mullet, spot and pompano, and a chunk of mullet on a fish-finder rig for a red or a bluefish. The October 1 report had slot and over-slot reds, Spanish and bluefish along the beach and pompano and Virginia mullet on the bottom; the Topsail.Fish October 21 summary puts sea mullet and spot in the surf troughs and the piers, better toward evening. No source ties the Topsail surf bite to a tide stage, so take the ocean times as they are. Sunday the rip current risk goes to High with 3 to 5 foot surf and gusts to 31, and it stays High through Wednesday; Thursday and Friday, sunny and 75, look like the week's surf days. Do not wade past your knees today.

The piers. The piers post their daily catches only on Facebook, which this report cannot read, so the written word is three weeks old. From the October 1 report: Jolly Roger had the king bite on with fish to 32 pounds, Spanish getting bigger, and night bottom fishing best for red drum to 31 inches, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish and black drum; Surf City Pier had good-sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot reds and evening bottom bags of black drum, mullet, spot and croaker; Seaview said "the mullet migration is starting", with Spanish and bluefish on Gotchas or diamond jigs and a bottom mix of sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, speckled trout and black drum. The Topsail.Fish summaries of October 14 and 21 repeat the Jolly Roger kings and call spot "developing, not yet in full swing". With the wind along the beach and gusts near 30 after dark, this is a bottom-rig night, not a king-rig night: bloodworms, shrimp or Fishbites on a two-hook rig, and the evening tide tops at 6:41 PM. For today's word, call: Jolly Roger 910-328-4616, Surf City 910-328-3521, Seaview 910-328-3172. Surf City Pier is open 24 hours and the grill 7 AM to midnight on a Saturday (pier site, read today), $12 for one rod or $24 for two, good 24 hours, blanket licence included (fees page, October 23); Jolly Roger is $16 a day, kids 13 and under half price, blanket licence too, and its grill serves breakfast and lunch Friday to Sunday 7 AM to 2 PM (pier pages, read today). Seaview is $17 a pass (pier house page, October 23).

Wrightsville Beach, the nearest busy reporting area, 25 miles south. Its Fisherman's Post October report (October 1; nothing newer on the index today) had trout "picking up" in the creeks, topwater at first light and then live mullet in 4 to 6 feet, reds at the docks on both rising and falling tides and bigger fish on the jetties on whole or cut mullet, sheepshead on deep docks at low tide on mud crabs, Spanish 1 to 3 miles out, kings to 33 pounds from Johnnie Mercer's Pier, and false albacore 5 to 8 miles out and expected closer as the water cools. Captain Jot Owens' October outlook on his own site, a forecast rather than a catch report, calls October his biggest trout month of the year, on MirrOlure 17MR and 52MR plugs, Gulp shrimp and live shrimp under a cork, with Spanish and false albacore around the inlets and nearshore reefs under diving birds, on small spoons and light spinning tackle. Nothing dated this week was found for false albacore off either beach; nobody is getting out to look.

Regs Corner

  • One new proclamation this week, and it is commercial: FF-47-2026, signed October 23, opens the commercial flounder fishery in the Atlantic Ocean from October 27. It changes nothing for rod-and-reel anglers. Nothing else new on the current proclamations index (all six pages read today) since FF-45-2026 and FF-46-2026 on October 20, both offshore snapper-grouper rules.
  • Shellfish waters: both sounds open. The newest item on the polluted-area list today is PA-67-2026 (Brunswick and Carteret only, October 17); Stump Sound has been open since October 16 (PA-66-2026) and Topsail Sound since October 9 (PA-61-2026). With no rain in the forecast they should stay open; the 24 hour hotline is 252-515-5656.
  • Oysters are in season in open waters: hand harvest from public bottom, 1 bushel per person per day, 2 per boat, 3 inch minimum, cull on site, hands or hand rakes only, sunrise to sunset, sanctuaries off limits (SF-1-2026). No licence is needed for recreational shellfish.
  • Flounder: closed. The 2026 season was September 1 to 14 only (FF-27-2026). Release every one, including the ones that eat your red drum bait.
  • Red drum: one a day, 18 to 27 inch slot. Speckled trout: three a day, 14 to 20 inch slot, one over 26 allowed. Black drum: ten a day, 14 to 25 inches, one over 25 allowed. Sheepshead: five a day, 14 inch minimum. Bluefish: five a day. Spot: fifty a day. Striped bass from the sound, the waterway or the New River cannot be kept at all. If you keep a red, a trout or a weakfish, report it at deq.nc.gov/report-my-fish.
