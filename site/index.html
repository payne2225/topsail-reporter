The Bite

Best bet: The drains north of the inlet at the 6:43 AM sunrise, while the water is off the grass until 7:45 AM, cut mullet for reds, then live shrimp for trout on the flood.

The sound

  • WhenFlood from the 4:57 AM low to the 11:29 AM high. Out of the grass until 7:45 AM, and again 2:10 PM to 9:34 PM.
  • WhereDrains and oyster bars at dawn; the deep seams, 5 to 8 feet, in the creeks off Banks Channel on the flood.
  • BaitLive shrimp under a cork or a 3/16 oz jig with a soft plastic for trout; cut mullet on a Carolina rig for reds.
  • LatestSmall trout thick, some to 28 inches, on floated shrimp; reds scattered, cut bait beating plastics (Fisherman's Post, Nov 1). Nothing newer is posted.

The inlet

  • WhenFlood to the 10:36 AM high; north 15 to 20 knots early, seas 2 to 3 feet.
  • LatestBluefish, false albacore and gray trout on nearshore structure, diamond or epoxy jigs (Fisherman's Post, Nov 1).

The surf

  • WhenFlood to the 10:19 AM high, ebb to the 4:55 PM low; offshore wind, chop 1 to 3 feet, water 67.
  • BaitShrimp on a two-hook rig for whiting; a Gotcha plug for blues at first light.
  • LatestOccasional trout in the surf; Spanish and blues slowed by wind (Fisherman's Post, Nov 1).

The piers

  • WhenBlues at first light, bottom fish after dark.
  • LatestJolly Roger scattered trout, mullet and spot at night, blues early; Surf City pompano, black drum, croaker (Fisherman's Post, Nov 1).

Tip

A 42 degree dawn pushes trout into the deepest bend of a creek. Fish slow: let the jig rest on bottom a count of three before each lift.
